# Gateway Interview Presentation

## Presentation Positioning

**Project:** Gateway, Richcraft Homes  
**Format:** 8-10 minute interview presentation  
**Role:** Brand Designer / Visual System / Launch Rollout  
**Tools:** Figma / Illustrator / InDesign / Photoshop / After Effects / Premiere Pro  
**Core story:** Gateway needed to speak to younger first-time buyers without sounding like a traditional real estate campaign. I helped build a brand system that felt modern, warm, and scalable across launch touchpoints.

**One-line takeaway**
I helped turn a new residential community into a clear, approachable brand system for first-time buyers, then carried that system across print, digital, email, social, and on-site campaign assets.

**What I want interviewers to remember**
This project shows how I connect brand strategy, visual judgment, and production systems. I was not only making assets look polished. I was translating a business goal into a visual language that could work across the whole launch.

**Speaker notes**
Gateway was a new Richcraft Homes residential community in Kanata, a suburb of Ottawa. The audience was younger than the typical move-up buyer: mostly millennials and Gen Z buyers entering the housing market for the first time. My work focused on the brand rollout, visual system, and launch assets across print, digital, social, email, and signage.

Richcraft is an established builder, but Gateway had a different audience from many of their previous communities. This was not aimed at people upgrading into a larger home. It was aimed at people trying to find a way into the market. That changed the tone of the brand. It needed to feel trustworthy, but not formal. Polished, but not intimidating.

Gateway needed to feel:

- Warm

- Like a beginning

**Suggested visual**

When you look across residential marketing, much of the category uses the same visual language: serif type, gold accents, and lifestyle photography that can feel more cheesy than personal. ## Slide 4 - Design Direction

**On-slide copy**

# Modern simplicity.
## Every element had to earn its place.

Design principles:

- Simple type hierarchy
- Restrained color use
- Generous white space
- Architecture-led palette
- Natural, lived-in photography
- Flexible layout rules

Photography direction:

**Type**
Editorial suburban lifestyle photography

- Real people in relaxed, everyday moments
- Warm natural light and soft shadows
- Architectural textures like concrete, stone, glass, and greenery
- Crops that feel personal, not staged
- Images that suggest a lifestyle without feeling like stock real estate

**Suggested visual**
`../../assets/gateway/branding_moodboard.png`

**Speaker notes**
The direction became modern simplicity. I wanted the brand to feel confident without trying too hard. That meant reducing visual noise: fewer type styles, a tight palette, and layouts with room to breathe. The photography followed the same logic. I would describe the style as editorial suburban lifestyle photography: candid-feeling people, warm natural light, and architectural textures like concrete, stone, and greenery. Instead of overly staged real estate imagery, the images needed to feel personal and connected to everyday first-home living in Kanata. The goal was to create clarity for the buyer and consistency for the marketing team.

**Transition**
From there, I translated the strategy into a system that could survive real launch conditions.

## Slide 10 - What I Learned

**On-slide copy**

# The quieter the system, the louder the brand.

What changed in my thinking:

- More options do not always create more flexibility.
- A tighter system makes production faster.
- White space can communicate confidence.
- A brand feels stronger when it knows who it is not trying to impress.

**Suggested visual**
Use a clean text slide, or pair with a calm detail crop from the brochure/social system.

**Speaker notes**
At the beginning, I thought flexibility meant giving the brand more elements. By the end, the work became stronger when I stripped it back: fewer colors, fewer type treatments, and clearer layout rules. That restraint made the brand easier to recognize and easier for the team to use.

## Short 3-Minute Version

Use this if the interviewer asks for a quick walkthrough.

**Opening**
Gateway was a Richcraft Homes residential community aimed at younger first-time buyers. The challenge was that most real estate branding speaks in a formal, luxury-coded language, while this audience needed something clearer, warmer, and less intimidating.

**Problem**
The brand needed to feel like a beginning, not a prestige purchase. It also needed to work across many launch touchpoints: print, social, email, signage, and sales center materials.

**Process**
Instead of starting with a generic moodboard, I used the actual architectural renderings as the foundation. The clean building lines, warm materials, and open spaces shaped the palette, typography, and layout rules.

**Solution**
I helped build a restrained visual system using warm neutral tones, clean typography, generous white space, and repeatable templates. The system carried across brochures, floor plans, social ads, email campaigns, motion assets, and signage.

**Outcome**
Gateway reached the audience it was built for: 600+ registrants from the target demographic, 4,100+ new users, and a 35% conversion rate from registration interest to confirmed action.

**Learning**
The biggest lesson was that restraint can make a brand stronger. By reducing the number of visual choices, the system became clearer, more recognizable, and easier to use.

## Interview Q&A Prep

### Why did you choose this project for an interview?

Because it shows both visual craft and systems thinking. It was not just one polished brand moment. It required a consistent identity across many practical launch assets.

### What was the hardest part?

Balancing approachability with credibility. The brand had to feel younger and more modern, but it still needed to feel trustworthy enough for a major home purchase.

### What was your strongest design decision?

Starting from the architecture. It kept the brand grounded in the real community and avoided a generic real estate look.

### What would you improve if you had more time?

I would formalize the system into a more complete brand toolkit with clearer usage documentation, componentized social templates, and performance learnings from each channel.

### How did you collaborate?

I worked within a larger launch context, aligning the visual system with marketing needs and production requirements. The work involved translating the brand direction into usable assets across teams and formats.

### What does this project say about you as a designer?

It shows that I can move between concept and execution. I care about the idea behind the work, but I also care about whether the system can actually be used, updated, and scaled.
