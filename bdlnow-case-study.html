Making Canada's real-time GDP estimate readable by anyone

BDLNow is the Business Data Lab's weekly GDP nowcast for Canada. A nowcast estimates economic growth in real time, before official data is published. Powered by 45 indicators, updated every Monday, months ahead of Statistics Canada. I designed the interface and integrated the R Shiny application into the live WordPress site.

Role Product Designer (UI, UX) · Marketing Designer
Tools Figma · WordPress · Illustrator · Photoshop · After Effects
Service Data Visualization

The Problem Research Design Outcome Learned
01 — The Problem

The model was ready.
Nobody outside the team could read it.

Official GDP data from Statistics Canada takes about 60 days to be published. By then, business leaders and policymakers have already made critical hiring and investment decisions based on partial signal. My job was to take our economists' model and turn it into something a journalist, policymaker, or CEO could read and trust in seconds.

01

The 60-Day Blind Spot

Stakeholders were forced to make fast-moving economic calls on lagging indicators. The official number always arrived after the decision was already made.

02

Academic Friction

The existing reference data required statistical context that most casual readers did not have. The model was credible. The presentation assumed expertise the audience did not bring.

03

The "Invisible" Answers

Every time our economists shared early data, stakeholders asked the same thing: what does this mean? The model had the answers. The interface hid them.

The Design Mission

The model was built for economists. The platform had to work for everyone else.

02 — Research

Comparative Audit

I pulled in colleagues from across the Chamber. Policy advisors, communications staff, membership managers. I put the New York Fed's nowcast in front of them and asked them to find the GDP estimate.

Most took well over 30 seconds just to locate the GDP estimate. And even when they found it, the probability ranges left them unsure whether the number meant anything.

The NY Fed model was our benchmark. It showed what a public-facing GDP nowcast could look like. BDLNow was built to do the same thing for Canada, and actually be readable.

01 Flaw 01

Hidden on Hover

The estimate is a dot on the chart, the same visual weight as every other point. Most people scrolled past it without knowing it was there.

02 Flaw 02

Number Without Meaning

Four probability bands, a bell curve, raw interval ranges stacked on top of each other. Precise, but unreadable.

The NY Fed Staff Nowcast. The same model BDLNow runs on, and the interface we set out to rethink.

The UX Challenge

The NY Fed proved the model could be public. BDLNow had to prove it could also be readable.

From that audit, I mapped every friction point to a direct design response. Same model. The interface is completely different because the reader is completely different.

NY Fed Friction

The estimate is buried in text or revealed only through chart interaction.

Probability bands require statistical confidence to understand.

The model's credibility is not obvious to non-experts.

Design Angle

Make the answer visible before asking users to interpret the model.

Replace statistical abstraction with a consensus frame.

Let users judge the model's track record in one view.

03 — Design

Conclusion first, context second.

That one rule drove every decision. The answer is on screen the moment the page loads. The data behind it is one click away.

It's how the three design angles from the audit became a real interface: the estimate labeled on the chart, the Bloomberg range standing in for statistical confidence, and the model's track record visible in one view.

Inside the platform

Three sections. Each answering a different question.

Estimate vs consensus, always visible

BDLNow's line and the Bloomberg professional range in one chart. No toggling.

Hover for the exact weekly value

Tooltip shows the estimate and date for each data point.

Direction readable at a glance

Full quarter in view on load. No clicks needed to read the trend.

Grouped by category, not by data series

Consumption, Production, Financial, Labour. Named for what they mean, not what Stats Canada calls them.

Data Release Impact table

Sits below the bar chart. Each row is an indicator. Expand any row to see the description, transformation method, and Stats Canada source.

Past and present in one view

Light blue is official GDP. Purple is BDLNow's estimate. No switching needed.

Dots show past accuracy

Each dot is BDLNow's estimate before the official number came out. Model track record, visible in the chart.

04 — Outcome

Live, public, and updated every week.

businessdatalab.ca/bdlnow

BDLNow is the first freely available public nowcast of Canadian GDP. It launched in September 2025 and has been running every Monday since. No editorial adjustment. No paywall.

~60 days

Ahead of Statistics Canada's official release. Business leaders and policymakers get a credible estimate while the official number is still being calculated.

Every
Monday

Markets and decisions don't wait for quarterly reports. A fresh estimate every week means the number stays current as conditions change.

45

Indicators across retail, employment, housing, commodities, and financial markets. More sources means no single data release can throw off the whole estimate.

05 — What I Learned

What this project
taught me.

Understanding the model was part of the design work. I sat with the economists until I could explain what moved the number each week and what the Bloomberg range actually meant. That knowledge changed decisions I made on screen. You cannot design the communication of something you do not understand.

BDLNow gets read by people making decisions and writing headlines. I couldn't just put data on a screen and hope they figured it out. The top of the page had to be clear enough that anyone landing there for the first time understood exactly what the number meant, where it came from, and why they should trust it.
